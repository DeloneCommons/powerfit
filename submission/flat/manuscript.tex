\title{Fixed-site inverse fitting of power-diagram weights from pairwise separator data}

\author[aff1]{Ivan Yu. Chernyshov\corref{cor1}}
\ead{chernyshov@pish.itmo.ru}

\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author}
\address[aff1]{Advanced Engineering School, ITMO University, St. Petersburg 191002, Russia}

\begin{abstract}
Power diagrams partition space by fixed sites and scalar weights. We study the
fixed-site inverse problem with sparse separator observations on selected site
pairs. Each observation is affine in a weight difference, so exact compatibility
and identifiability on a fixed row set reduce to cycle consistency and
connectivity of a row-indexed constraint multigraph. With noisy data, the
quadratic estimator is a gauge-fixed weighted Laplacian solve; hard equality
and interval restrictions reduce to classical difference constraints. Algebraic
consistency does not ensure that fitted pairs are realized as faces. We
therefore add connector-visible support diagnostics: a local visibility
criterion, a clearance formula, perturbation bounds, a box-clipping extension,
and a one-step fit--check--prune result under a margin condition. Synthetic
tests validate exact recovery, noise and missing-row behavior, hard-constraint
feasibility, periodic image handling, and active-support cleanup. As an
external benchmark, we use molecular support from the Quantum Theory of Atoms in
Molecules (QTAIM). Projected bond critical points provide pairwise separator
proxies, with off-axis distance as a proxy-quality flag. On 100 molecules, a
frozen proatomic separator law closes 75.7\% of the midpoint-to-ceiling gap on
QTAIM-supported pairs. From a generic 5.0~\AA{} candidate graph,
visibility-masked recovered support reaches F1 $=0.821$ overall and $0.9997$ on
bonded edges. On visible nonbonded faces, clearance ranks QTAIM support better
for fitted power diagrams than for Voronoi diagrams (average precision 0.830
versus 0.707). The molecular claim is limited to pairwise support and separator
diagnostics, not full Bader partition reconstruction.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Power diagrams (Laguerre tessellations) partition space from fixed
sites and scalar weights. Many inverse settings observe richer data---cell
volumes, centroids, fuller boundary geometry, or large pieces of an already
segmented tessellation. Here the sites are fixed and the data consist only of
sparse separator observations on selected site pairs. The question is whether
those local observations admit a globally consistent weighted surrogate, how
identifiable that surrogate is, and which candidate pairs are actually
realized as faces.

This sparse pairwise regime isolates the part of the inverse problem that is
genuinely about reconciling local separator evidence on a candidate graph. On a
fixed row set, separator observations induce implied weight differences on a
constraint multigraph, so exact compatibility becomes an incidence- and
cycle-consistency question. With noise, the same structure yields gauge-aware
inverse fits with confidence weights and optional hard interval or equality
restrictions. What it does not guarantee is geometric realization of the same
rows as faces. We therefore separate an inner fixed-row layer from an outer
realization-aware layer that checks realized support and prunes inconsistent
rows.

The scope is intentionally narrow. We study a fixed-site sparse inverse
formulation, develop explicit local diagnostics for connector-visible support,
and test a realization-aware workflow on a molecular benchmark derived from the
Quantum Theory of Atoms in Molecules (QTAIM). In that benchmark, QTAIM supplies
a nontrivial molecular support graph and projected bond critical points (BCPs)
as separator proxies; it is not treated as a target for full Bader reconstruction.
Figure~\ref{fig:workflow_overview} summarizes the split between sparse local
observations, fixed-row inverse fitting, realization checks, and active-support
cleanup.

The main contributions are as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item a fixed-row inverse theory for sparse separator observations,
    including affine row laws, cycle-consistency compatibility, gauge and
    identifiability on the effective site graph, and feasibility of hard
    interval or equality restrictions;
    \item connector-visible support diagnostics that turn part of the
    realization question into explicit local formulas: a visibility criterion,
    a clearance score, a clipped-box extension, perturbation bounds, and a
    one-step fit--check--prune result under a margin condition;
    \item a realization-aware workflow that combines the inner inverse solve
    with periodic-image handling, full face matching, and active-support
    refinement when candidate rows are uncertain; and
    \item synthetic controls and a QTAIM-derived molecular benchmark that test
    exact recovery, noisy and missing rows, infeasible restrictions, supergraph
    cleanup, separator quality on true support, and recovery from a generic
    excessive graph.
\end{itemize}

Classical treatments of power diagrams and their dual regular
triangulations establish the affine separator structure used here and the
broader algorithmic setting of Laguerre geometry
\cite{Aurenhammer1987,Aurenhammer1991,EdelsbrunnerShah1996,LautensackZuyev2008}.
Inverse and reconstruction-oriented studies often assume richer observables,
such as fuller boundary geometry, cell volumes, or centroids, and
recognition-oriented work asks when a given cell complex or bisector pattern is
Voronoi-realizable at all
\cite{Schoenberg2003,Hartvigsen1992,Duan2014,Spettl2016,Petrich2019,Bourne2025}.
The regime studied here is deliberately narrower: sparse local separator data
on a row-indexed candidate set, with fixed sites and unknown weights.

Within a fixed support, the resulting row-difference problem is naturally read
as an edge-flow or pairwise-difference model on a graph, where cycle
inconsistency obstructs the existence of a global potential
\cite{Jiang2011}. Hard interval or equality restrictions fit the classical
difference-constraint framework \cite{Bellman1958}. Solving for power-diagram
weights also appears in least-squares clustering and semi-discrete optimal
transport \cite{AurenhammerHoffmannAronov1998,KitagawaMerigotThibert2019}, but
those formulations infer weights from global cell observables rather than from
sparse pairwise separator data.

The application-side validation is likewise focused. The molecular benchmark
uses QTAIM molecular-graph support extracted from electronic wavefunctions,
with topology analysis carried out through Multiwfn
\cite{LuChen2012,Lu2024Multiwfn}. Projected bond critical points serve as
pairwise separator proxies, and off-axis distance is treated only as a quality
flag; the benchmark is therefore about pairwise support rather than full Bader
partition recovery \cite{Bader1990}. Experiment~J1 makes the heterogeneity
explicit: bonded BCPs are highly connector-aligned, whereas nonbonded BCPs are
often far off-axis and are interpreted as a harsher support-diagnostic stress
test. Lane et al. are cited only for the limited caveat that absence of a bond
critical point does not automatically imply absence of a chemically meaningful
weak interaction \cite{Lane2013}.

Accordingly, the paper has three layers. The fixed-row inverse problem is
theorem-backed. The connector-visible support diagnostic used in the molecular
study is also theorem-backed: once sites and fitted weights are fixed,
visibility and clearance reduce to explicit row-local quantities. The remaining
algorithmic layer couples those diagnostics to full tessellation matching,
periodic-image bookkeeping, and active-set updates. Algebraic compatibility of
a row set does not imply full face realization, but it does support explicit
local support scores that can be analyzed mathematically.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:background} defines the geometry and observation model.
Section~\ref{sec:consistency} gives exact consistency and identifiability
results. Section~\ref{sec:inversefit} introduces the optimization models.
Section~\ref{sec:refinement} presents connector-visible support diagnostics and
self-consistent refinement. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports numerical
experiments, and Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses scope and limitations.

\section{Background and problem formulation}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Sites, weights, and power separators}
Let $p_1,\dots,p_n \in \R^d$ be fixed sites and let $w_1,\dots,w_n \in \R$ be
weights. Define the power function of site $i$ by
\begin{equation}
    \pi_i(x) = \lVert x-p_i \rVert^2 - w_i.
\end{equation}
In the periodic case, fix one specific image $p_j^{\star}$ of site $j$.

\begin{proposition}[Affine pair law]
\label{prop:affine}
Fix a pair $(i,j)$ and, in the periodic case, a specific image $p_j^{\star}$ of
$p_j$. Let
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{ij} = p_j^{\star}-p_i,
    \qquad
    d_{ij} = \lVert \Delta_{ij} \rVert > 0,
\end{equation}
and parameterize the connector line by
\begin{equation}
    x(t) = p_i + t\,\Delta_{ij},
    \qquad t\in\R.
\end{equation}
Then the two-site power separator $\pi_i(x)=\pi_j(x)$ intersects this line at the
unique parameter
\begin{equation}
    t_{ij} = \frac12 + \frac{w_i-w_j}{2d_{ij}^2}.
\end{equation}
Hence the normalized separator fraction is affine in $w_i-w_j$. If the
observable is the absolute position measured from $p_i$ along the connector,
$x_{ij}=d_{ij}t_{ij}$, then
\begin{equation}
    x_{ij} = \frac{d_{ij}}{2} + \frac{w_i-w_j}{2d_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, for either supported measurement model,
\begin{equation}
    y_{ij}=\beta_{ij}+\alpha_{ij}(w_i-w_j),
\end{equation}
with $(\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij})=(1/(2d_{ij}^2),1/2)$ for fraction measurements and
$(\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij})=(1/(2d_{ij}),d_{ij}/2)$ for position measurements.
Any affine reparameterization of these observables is therefore also affine in
$w_i-w_j$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta=\Delta_{ij}=p_j^{\star}-p_i$ and $d=d_{ij}=\lVert \Delta \rVert>0$.
By definition, the pairwise power separator is the set of points $x$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
    \lVert x-p_i \rVert^2 - w_i = \lVert x-p_j^{\star} \rVert^2 - w_j.
\end{equation}
Restrict this equality to the connector line $x(t)=p_i+t\Delta$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \lVert x(t)-p_i \rVert^2 = t^2 d^2,
    \qquad
    \lVert x(t)-p_j^{\star} \rVert^2 = (1-t)^2 d^2.
\end{equation}
Substituting into the separator equation gives
\begin{equation}
    t^2 d^2 - w_i = (1-t)^2 d^2 - w_j.
\end{equation}
Expanding and rearranging,
\begin{equation}
    \bigl(t^2-(1-2t+t^2)\bigr)d^2 = w_i-w_j,
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
    (2t-1)d^2 = w_i-w_j.
\end{equation}
Because $d>0$, this has the unique solution
\begin{equation}
    t = \frac12 + \frac{w_i-w_j}{2d^2}.
\end{equation}
This is the stated fraction formula. Multiplying by $d$ yields the position
formula
\begin{equation}
    x_{ij}=dt = \frac d2 + \frac{w_i-w_j}{2d}.
\end{equation}
The coefficient pairs $(\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij})$ follow immediately. Any affine
reparameterization of $t_{ij}$ or $x_{ij}$ remains affine in $w_i-w_j$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Constraint rows, site graph, realized rows, and the effective graph}
Let $\rowset=\{1,\dots,m\}$ be the index set of measured or hypothesized
separator rows. Each row $r\in\rowset$ carries
\begin{equation}
    (i_r,j_r,s_r,y_r,\alpha_r,\beta_r,\lambda_r),
\end{equation}
where $i_r,j_r\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $i_r\neq j_r$ are site indices,
$s_r\in\mathbb{Z}^d$ is a periodic shift label (equal to $0$ in the
nonperiodic case), $y_r$ is the observed separator quantity,
$(\alpha_r,\beta_r)$ are the affine coefficients from \cref{prop:affine} for
the requested image of site $j_r$, and $\lambda_r\ge 0$ is an optional
confidence or precision weight used in inverse fitting. Distinct rows may
involve the same unordered site pair with different shifts or repeated
measurements.

The primary combinatorial object is therefore the row-indexed constraint
multigraph
\begin{equation}
    \cgraph=(V,\rowset),
    \qquad
    V=\{1,\dots,n\},
\end{equation}
whose $r$th edge joins $i_r$ and $j_r$. Parallel edges are allowed. By
forgetting row multiplicity and shifts we obtain the site graph
\begin{equation}
    \sitegraph=(V,\candpairs),
    \qquad
    \candpairs=\bigl\{\{i_r,j_r\}: r\in\rowset\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
In nonperiodic settings, or whenever each unordered site pair appears at most
once, $\cgraph$ and $\sitegraph$ carry the same information. In periodic
settings they need not: different rows can constrain the same site pair through
different requested images.

For a fitted weight vector $\weights$ and a chosen domain $D$, define the
realized row set $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{row}}(\weights)\subseteq\rowset$ by
declaring that $r\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{row}}(\weights)$ if the cell of site
$i_r$ and the cell of the shifted image of site $j_r$ indexed by $s_r$ are both
nonempty and their common boundary inside $D$ has positive $(d-1)$-dimensional
measure. The realized site-pair set is then
\begin{equation}
    \realpairs(\weights)
    =
    \bigl\{\{i_r,j_r\}: r\in\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{row}}(\weights)\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
A row is counted as realized only when the requested shift $s_r$ is realized;
if some other shift of site $j_r$ is realized instead, that is a separate
diagnostic outcome but not membership in
$\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{row}}(\weights)$.

Rows with zero confidence do not affect the inverse objective or
identifiability. We therefore define the informative row set
\begin{equation}
    \rowset_+ = \{r\in\rowset : \lambda_r>0\}
\end{equation}
and the corresponding effective site graph
\begin{equation}
    \effgraph=(V,E_{\mathrm{eff}}),
    \qquad
    E_{\mathrm{eff}}=\bigl\{\{i_r,j_r\}: r\in\rowset_+\bigr\}.
\end{equation}
Whenever all rows are informative, $\effgraph=\sitegraph$. The theorem layer
below is stated on a fixed row set, while identifiability statements are
understood on $\effgraph$ when zero-confidence rows are allowed.

\subsection{Observation models}
We use two equivalent rowwise observation models throughout the paper:
\begin{itemize}
    \item separator fraction along the segment joining $p_{i_r}$ to the
    requested image of $p_{j_r}$;
    \item projected separator position or signed offset along the same
    connector.
\end{itemize}
Raw separator observations can be reduced to one of these forms by projecting
separator information onto the connector joining the relevant site/image pair.
After this reduction, every row obeys an affine law
\begin{equation}
    y_r=\beta_r+\alpha_r(w_{i_r}-w_{j_r}),
    \qquad r\in\rowset,
    \label{eq:row_affine}
\end{equation}
where $(\alpha_r,\beta_r)$ depend on the measurement convention and, in
periodic settings, on the requested shift $s_r$. Table~\ref{tab:notation}
collects the recurring row-wise symbols used in the sequel.

\subsection{Gauge freedom and inverse objective}
Only weight differences across informative rows matter. Therefore $\weights$ is
identifiable only up to one additive constant per connected component of the
effective site graph.

\begin{proposition}[Gauge freedom and identifiability]
\label{prop:gauge}
Consider the rowwise observation model
\begin{equation}
    y_r=\beta_r+\alpha_r(w_{i_r}-w_{j_r}),
    \qquad r\in\rowset_+,
    \label{eq:gauge_rows}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_r\neq 0$. If the effective site graph $\effgraph$ has
$c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ connected components $V_1,\dots,V_{c_{\mathrm{eff}}}$, then
the inverse problem is identifiable only up to one additive constant per
component. Equivalently, weight differences are identifiable on each connected
component of $\effgraph$, but the absolute level of each component is not.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
First fix arbitrary constants $\gamma_1,\dots,\gamma_{c_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ and
define a new weight vector $\weights'$ by
\begin{equation}
    w_i' = w_i + \gamma_k, \qquad i\in V_k.
\end{equation}
If $r\in\rowset_+$, then $i_r$ and $j_r$ lie in the same connected component of
$\effgraph$ because the row itself contributes the edge $\{i_r,j_r\}$ to
$E_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    w_{i_r}'-w_{j_r}' = (w_{i_r}+\gamma_k)-(w_{j_r}+\gamma_k)=w_{i_r}-w_{j_r},
\end{equation}
and \eqref{eq:gauge_rows} gives the same predicted observation for $\weights$
and $\weights'$. This proves the gauge freedom.

Conversely, suppose $\weights$ and $\widetilde{\weights}$ produce the same
predicted observations on all informative rows. Since $\alpha_r\neq 0$, equality
of observations implies
\begin{equation}
    \widetilde{w}_{i_r}-\widetilde{w}_{j_r}=w_{i_r}-w_{j_r},
    \qquad r\in\rowset_+.
\end{equation}
Define $\delta_i=\widetilde{w}_i-w_i$. Then
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{i_r}-\delta_{j_r}=0,
    \qquad r\in\rowset_+.
\end{equation}
Thus $\delta$ is constant along every edge of $\effgraph$, and therefore
constant on each connected component of $\effgraph$. Hence
$\widetilde{\weights}-\weights$ consists exactly of one additive constant per
effective connected component, and no more can be identified from the
informative rows.
\end{proof}

\section{Exact consistency and identifiability}
\label{sec:consistency}

\subsection{Row-difference representation}
Using \cref{prop:affine}, convert each exact row observation into an implied row
difference
\begin{equation}
    z_r^{\mathrm{obs}} = \frac{y_r-\beta_r}{\alpha_r},
    \qquad r\in\rowset.
\end{equation}
Then exact compatibility on the chosen row set is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
    z_r^{\mathrm{obs}} = w_{i_r}-w_{j_r},
    \qquad r\in\rowset.
\end{equation}
Orient every row from $i_r$ to $j_r$, and let
$\inc\in\R^{n\times m}$ be the corresponding incidence matrix whose $r$th column
is $e_{i_r}-e_{j_r}$. Then exact compatibility is the linear system
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}} = \inc^\top\weights.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Row compatibility and multigraph cycle consistency]
\label{thm:cycle}
Fix a row set $\rowset=\{1,\dots,m\}$ and let
$\inc\in\R^{n\times m}$ be the row-incidence matrix of the constraint
multigraph $\cgraph$. For the implied row data
$\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}\in\R^m$, the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item There exists a weight vector $\weights\in\R^n$ such that
    \begin{equation}
        z_r^{\mathrm{obs}}=w_{i_r}-w_{j_r},
        \qquad r\in\rowset.
    \end{equation}
    \item $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}\in\mathrm{Im}(\inc^\top)$.
    \item $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}\perp\ker(\inc)$; equivalently, every
    signed cycle sum in the constraint multigraph $\cgraph$ vanishes.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold, the solution set is an affine space of dimension
$c$, where $c$ is the number of connected components of the site graph
$\sitegraph$. More precisely, if $V_1,\dots,V_c$ are those components and
$\weights_0$ is one solution, then
\begin{equation}
    \{\weights:\inc^\top\weights=\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}\}
    =
    \weights_0+\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}_{V_1},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{V_c}\}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Statements (1) and (2) are identical once the incidence matrix is defined. In
finite dimensions,
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Im}(\inc^\top)=\ker(\inc)^\perp,
\end{equation}
so (2) and (3) are equivalent. For an incidence matrix of a multigraph,
$\ker(\inc)$ is the cycle space: it is generated by signed indicator vectors of
cycles, including $2$-cycles formed by parallel edges. Hence orthogonality to
$\ker(\inc)$ is exactly the vanishing of every signed cycle sum.

If $\weights_0$ is one solution, then all solutions are of the form
$\weights_0+\delta$ with $\delta\in\ker(\inc^\top)$. Now
\begin{equation}
    \delta\in\ker(\inc^\top)
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \delta_{i_r}-\delta_{j_r}=0
    \text{ for every } r\in\rowset.
\end{equation}
Thus $\delta$ is constant along every edge of the site graph $\sitegraph$, and
therefore constant on each connected component $V_k$ of $\sitegraph$.
Conversely, any componentwise constant vector clearly lies in
$\ker(\inc^\top)$. This gives the stated affine solution set and dimension.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dimension count]
\label{cor:dimension}
If the site graph $\sitegraph$ has $n$ vertices, $m$ rows, and $c$ connected
components, then the space of exactly compatible row-difference data has
dimension $n-c$. Hence there are $m-n+c$ independent algebraic compatibility
conditions on the row data.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:cycle}, the exactly compatible row-difference fields are the
vectors in $\mathrm{Im}(\inc^\top)$, so it is enough to compute the rank of
$\inc$. Parallel columns do not change the rank, so $\operatorname{rank}(\inc)$
is the same as the rank of the ordinary incidence matrix of the site graph
$\sitegraph$. For a graph with $c$ connected components, that rank is $n-c$.
Therefore
\begin{equation}
    \dim \mathrm{Im}(\inc^\top)=\operatorname{rank}(\inc^\top)=n-c.
\end{equation}
Since the full row-data space has dimension $m$, its codimension inside
$\R^m$ is $m-(n-c)=m-n+c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Edge-flow viewpoint]
The row-difference field $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ is an edge-flow on the
constraint multigraph. In that language, exact compatibility is the existence
of a global potential whose edge differences reproduce the observed flow, while
nonzero signed cycle sums are the obstruction. This is the same mathematical
pattern that appears in pairwise-comparison and Hodge-theoretic graph models
\cite{Jiang2011}, although here the edge data come from separator geometry
rather than from ranking data.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}
In periodic settings, distinct rows can involve the same site pair with
different requested shifts. These appear as parallel edges of $\cgraph$. The
multigraph formulation therefore records an additional algebraic fact that a
simple site graph would hide: two such rows must imply the same weight
difference, which appears as a $2$-cycle consistency condition.
\end{remark}

\section{Inverse fitting models}
\label{sec:inversefit}

\subsection{Quadratic fit, Laplacian solve, and statistical reading}
Let $\rowset_+=\{r\in\rowset:\lambda_r>0\}$ be the informative rows, and let
$\inc_+\in\R^{n\times |\rowset_+|}$ be the incidence matrix obtained by
restricting the row-incidence matrix to $\rowset_+$. The basic inverse fit is
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\weights} \; \frac12 \sum_{r\in\rowset_+} \lambda_r
    \bigl(y_r^{\mathrm{obs}} - y_r(\weights)\bigr)^2,
    \label{eq:wls}
\end{equation}
subject to one gauge-fixing condition per connected component of the effective
site graph, for example $\sum_{i\in V_k} w_i = 0$ on each component $V_k$ of
$\effgraph$. Rows with $\lambda_r=0$ do not affect the fit or identifiability.

\begin{proposition}[Laplacian normal equations]
\label{prop:laplacian}
For each informative row define
\begin{equation}
    z_r^{\mathrm{obs}}=\frac{y_r^{\mathrm{obs}}-\beta_r}{\alpha_r},
    \qquad
    \rho_r=\lambda_r\alpha_r^2,
\end{equation}
and let $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}\in\R^{|\rowset_+|}$ collect the
$z_r^{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $\mathbf{R}=\mathrm{diag}(\rho_r)$. Then
\eqref{eq:wls} is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\weights}\;
    \frac12 \left\|
        \mathbf{R}^{1/2}\bigl(\inc_+^\top\weights-\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}\bigr)
    \right\|_2^2.
    \label{eq:wls_incidence}
\end{equation}
Its first-order conditions are
\begin{equation}
    \lap \weights = \inc_+ \mathbf{R}\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}},
    \qquad
    \lap = \inc_+ \mathbf{R}\inc_+^\top,
    \label{eq:normal_eqs}
\end{equation}
where $\lap$ is the weighted Laplacian of the effective site graph. If
$V_1,\dots,V_{c_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ are the connected components of $\effgraph$,
then
\begin{equation}
    \ker(\lap)=\mathrm{span}\{\mathbf{1}_{V_1},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{V_{c_{\mathrm{eff}}}}\},
\end{equation}
and the full solution set is
\begin{equation}
    \weights^\star = \lap^\dagger \inc_+ \mathbf{R}\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}
    + \sum_{k=1}^{c_{\mathrm{eff}}}\gamma_k \mathbf{1}_{V_k},
\end{equation}
with arbitrary $\gamma_k\in\R$. In particular, the minimizer is unique after
imposing one gauge condition per connected component of $\effgraph$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each informative row,
$y_r(\weights)=\beta_r+\alpha_r(w_{i_r}-w_{j_r})$ by
\cref{prop:affine}. Absorbing $\alpha_r$ into
$z_r^{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $\rho_r$ rewrites \eqref{eq:wls} as
\eqref{eq:wls_incidence}. Differentiating gives
$\inc_+\mathbf{R}(\inc_+^\top\weights-\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}})=0$, which is
exactly \eqref{eq:normal_eqs}. Finally,
\begin{equation}
    u^\top\lap u = \sum_{r\in\rowset_+}\rho_r(u_{i_r}-u_{j_r})^2 \ge 0,
\end{equation}
so $\lap$ is symmetric positive semidefinite, and its nullspace consists of the
vectors that are constant on each connected component of $\effgraph$. This is
precisely the gauge space from \cref{prop:gauge}, yielding the stated solution
family and uniqueness modulo one componentwise constant per connected
component.
\end{proof}

Under independent Gaussian row noise with variances $\sigma_r^2$,
maximizing the likelihood on the gauge-fixed subspace is equivalent to
\eqref{eq:wls} with $\lambda_r=\sigma_r^{-2}$. If
$Q\in\R^{n\times (n-c_{\mathrm{eff}})}$ has orthonormal columns spanning the
zero-mean gauge subspace and $\weights=Q\theta$, then the generalized
least-squares estimator is
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\theta}=(Q^\top\lap Q)^{-1}Q^\top \inc_+ \mathbf{R}\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}},
    \qquad
    \widehat{\weights}=Q\widehat{\theta},
\end{equation}
and every gauge-invariant contrast $a\in\mathcal{S}$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{Var}(a^\top\widehat{\weights}) = a^\top \lap^\dagger a.
\end{equation}
Thus the quadratic fit can be read simultaneously as a gauge-fixed Laplacian
solve and as generalized least squares on row differences.

\begin{remark}[Other weight-solving formulations]
Solving for power-diagram weights also appears in least-squares clustering and
in semi-discrete optimal transport, where the unknown weights are driven by
cell masses, capacities, or related global observables
\cite{AurenhammerHoffmannAronov1998,KitagawaMerigotThibert2019}. The present
setting is different: the sites are fixed, the observations are sparse pairwise
separator data on a chosen support, and the Laplacian system arises from
fitting edgewise weight differences rather than from enforcing cell-volume
constraints.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Projection viewpoint and disconnected components]
\label{rem:projection}
By \cref{thm:cycle}, the exactly compatible informative row-difference fields
are precisely the vectors in $\mathrm{Im}(\inc_+^\top)$. Therefore
\cref{prop:laplacian} shows that the quadratic inverse fit is the
$\mathbf{R}$-weighted orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ onto
$\mathrm{Im}(\inc_+^\top)$. The optimal residual measures the algebraic
distance of the informative row data from exact compatibility, while
disconnected informative graphs are handled componentwise rather than treated
as a pathology of the formulation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Robust losses and confidence weights}
The same row-difference structure extends beyond the quadratic case. Let $\mathcal{S}$ denote the
gauge-fixed subspace above, let $\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{S}$ be a nonempty
closed convex set encoding any additional linear restrictions, and consider
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\weights\in\mathcal{K}}
    \sum_{r\in\rowset_+}
    \lambda_r\,
    \varphi_r\!\left((\inc_+^\top\weights)_r-z_r^{\mathrm{obs}}\right),
    \label{eq:general_convex}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_r>0$ and each loss $\varphi_r:\R\to\R\cup\{+\infty\}$ is
proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous. The quadratic loss
$\varphi(r)=\tfrac12 r^2$, the absolute loss $\varphi(r)=|r|$, and Huber-type
piecewise quadratic-linear losses are the main examples relevant here; the last
of these is motivated by the classical robust-estimation construction of Huber
\cite{Huber1964}.

\begin{proposition}[Convexity, existence, and uniqueness of the inner fit]
\label{prop:convex}
Assume that each $\varphi_r$ in \eqref{eq:general_convex} is proper, convex,
lower semicontinuous, and coercive, and assume that the objective is proper on
$\mathcal{K}$; equivalently, there exists at least one $\weights\in\mathcal{K}$
for which all residual losses in \eqref{eq:general_convex} are finite. Then the
objective in \eqref{eq:general_convex} is a proper convex lower semicontinuous
function on $\mathcal{K}$ and the problem admits at least one minimizer. If,
in addition, every $\varphi_r$ is strictly convex, then the minimizer is
unique.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each residual map
$\weights\mapsto (\inc_+^\top\weights)_r-z_r^{\mathrm{obs}}$ is affine, so each
summand in \eqref{eq:general_convex} is convex and lower semicontinuous; the
same is therefore true of their positive weighted sum on $\mathcal{K}$. The
proper-on-$\mathcal{K}$ assumption rules out the degenerate extended-real case
in which every feasible point has objective value $+\infty$. On the gauge-fixed
subspace $\mathcal{S}$, the map $\inc_+^\top$ is injective by \cref{prop:gauge},
hence bounded below: there exists $\kappa>0$ such that
$\|\inc_+^\top\weights\|_2\ge \kappa\|\weights\|_2$ for all
$\weights\in\mathcal{S}$. Coercivity of the losses therefore makes the proper
objective coercive on $\mathcal{S}$, hence on the closed subset $\mathcal{K}$,
which yields existence. If every $\varphi_r$ is strictly convex, then
injectivity of $\inc_+^\top$ on $\mathcal{S}$ implies strict convexity of the
objective on $\mathcal{K}$ and therefore uniqueness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Hard interval and equality restrictions}
Typical hard restrictions include physically admissible separator locations,
known equalities for selected pair offsets, or application-specific allowable
fraction ranges. If an admissible observation interval on row $r$ is
\begin{equation}
    a_r\le y_r\le b_r,
\end{equation}
then the affine law $y_r=\beta_r+\alpha_r(w_{i_r}-w_{j_r})$ gives the
equivalent difference bounds
\begin{equation}
    \ell_r
    =
    \min\left\{
        \frac{a_r-\beta_r}{\alpha_r},
        \frac{b_r-\beta_r}{\alpha_r}
    \right\},
    \qquad
    u_r
    =
    \max\left\{
        \frac{a_r-\beta_r}{\alpha_r},
        \frac{b_r-\beta_r}{\alpha_r}
    \right\},
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
    \ell_r\le w_{i_r}-w_{j_r}\le u_r.
\end{equation}
Equality restrictions are the special case $\ell_r=u_r$. This is the classical
difference-constraint setting in graph form \cite{Bellman1958}.

\begin{theorem}[Feasibility of hard row restrictions]
\label{thm:feasibility}
Consider a system of hard row restrictions written in difference form as
\begin{equation}
    \ell_r \le w_{i_r}-w_{j_r} \le u_r,
    \qquad r\in\rowset,
    \label{eq:hard_bounds}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_r,u_r\in\R$ and $\ell_r\le u_r$. Equality restrictions are the
special case $\ell_r=u_r$. Construct a directed graph
$\graph_{\mathrm{dc}}=(V,A)$ by assigning, for each row $r\in\rowset$, the two
arcs
\begin{equation}
    j_r\to i_r \text{ with length } u_r,
    \qquad
    i_r\to j_r \text{ with length } -\ell_r.
    \label{eq:dc_arcs}
\end{equation}
Parallel arcs are allowed. Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The system \eqref{eq:hard_bounds} is feasible.
    \item The directed graph $\graph_{\mathrm{dc}}$ has no negative directed
    cycle.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, after adjoining a super-source $s$ with zero-length arcs
$s\to v$ for all $v\in V$, feasibility is equivalent to the absence of any
further Bellman--Ford relaxation after the standard $|V|$ full relaxation rounds
on the augmented graph. When the system is feasible, any shortest-path distance
vector from $s$ yields a feasible weight assignment. When the system is
infeasible, any negative cycle gives a contradiction witness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Each upper bound in \eqref{eq:hard_bounds} is equivalent to
$w_{i_r}\le w_{j_r}+u_r$, represented by the arc $j_r\to i_r$ of length
$u_r$, and each lower bound is equivalent to
$w_{j_r}\le w_{i_r}-\ell_r$, represented by the arc $i_r\to j_r$ of length
$-\ell_r$. The system is therefore exactly the family of arc inequalities
$w_v-w_u\le c_{uv}$ on $\graph_{\mathrm{dc}}$. Any feasible assignment makes the
sum of these inequalities around every directed cycle nonnegative, so negative
cycles are impossible. Conversely, if there is no negative directed cycle,
shortest-path distances from a zero super-source satisfy every arc inequality
and therefore every row bound. A negative cycle is thus an explicit
contradiction witness.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Closed versus strict restrictions]
\label{rem:strict}
The theorem is stated for closed interval or equality restrictions. In
applications, physically ``strict'' requirements such as a separator lying
strictly between the two sites are implemented numerically by replacing them
with a closed interval that includes a prescribed tolerance margin, for example
$t_r\in[\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon]$ in fraction space.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Practical solver stack}
In the archived implementation, the unconstrained quadratic model is assembled
componentwise as a dense Laplacian normal system and solved by direct dense
linear algebra, with one gauge fixed per unregularized connected component.
Hard interval or equality restrictions are prechecked first through the
directed difference-constraint reduction of \cref{thm:feasibility}, yielding a
compact contradiction witness when the admissible set is empty. The same
row-difference formulation is then used by the iterative solver path currently
implemented in the artifact for hard restrictions and for the nonquadratic
measurement-mismatch losses exposed there, namely squared and Huber loss.

For the quadratic fit, the residual
$\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}-\inc_+^\top\widehat{\weights}$ satisfies
$\inc_+ \mathbf{R}\mathbf{r}=0$. Thus
$\widehat{\mathbf{z}}=\inc_+^\top\widehat{\weights}$ is the
$\mathbf{R}$-orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}$ onto
$\mathrm{Im}(\inc_+^\top)$, and
$\|\mathbf{R}^{1/2}\mathbf{r}\|_2$ is a natural algebraic inconsistency index.
It vanishes exactly when the informative row data are compatible, equivalently
when the row set is multigraph-cycle consistent.

\section{Connector-visible support diagnostics and self-consistent refinement}
\label{sec:refinement}

The previous sections solve the \emph{inner} inverse problem on a fixed
row set. The remaining question is geometric: after fitting weights, which
candidate rows correspond to interfaces of the fitted power diagram? There are
two distinct support questions. The global one asks whether the full power face
is realized and, in periodic settings, with which image. That remains a
tessellation computation. The local one asks whether the connector line of a
pair passes through the relative interior of its fitted face inside the pair
bisector. The latter is exactly the diagnostic used later in
Experiment~J4, and unlike full face realization it admits an explicit
row-local theorem.

\subsection{Low-level inverse-fit workflow}
For a resolved constraint set, the low-level solver proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Resolve the pair geometry once: site indices, chosen periodic shifts,
    distances, and the affine coefficients $(\alpha_{ij},\beta_{ij})$ from
    \cref{prop:affine}.
    \item Convert hard interval or equality restrictions, if present, into
    bounds on weight differences and run the graph-based feasibility precheck of
    \cref{thm:feasibility}. If the bound system is infeasible, terminate
    early and return the contradiction witness.
    \item Build the effective graph of pair differences identified by the
    current objective and determine its connected components, which fixes the
    gauge structure from \cref{prop:gauge}.
    \item Solve the inner convex fit. In the purely quadratic case, assemble the
    componentwise Laplacian normal matrix from \cref{prop:laplacian} and solve
    it by direct dense linear algebra, fixing one gauge per unregularized
    component. In the archived implementation, nonquadratic mismatch is
    provided for squared and Huber losses, and hard restrictions use the same
    row-difference formulation through an iterative edge-separable solver.
    \item Recover predicted separator locations, measurement-space residuals,
    and a gauge-fixed radius representation for downstream tessellation calls.
\end{enumerate}

The software implementation follows exactly this decomposition. In particular,
the resolved pair object is reused across low-level fits, hard infeasibility is
detected before iterative optimization, and disconnected effective components
are handled by an explicit componentwise gauge policy rather than by anchor
order.

\subsection{Realized-face matching}
Given fitted weights, we may still need the full tessellation. The matching
stage computes the corresponding power diagram in the chosen domain and compares
the realized internal boundaries against the candidate rows. For each candidate
pair, it records whether the pair is realized at all, whether it is realized
with the requested periodic image, and, optionally, a boundary measure of the
matched interface (face area in three dimensions or edge length in two
dimensions). This cleanly separates algebraic residual diagnostics from global
geometric support diagnostics.

Explicit periodic-image bookkeeping is important. Candidate rows refer to one
requested image of the second site. Realization matching therefore checks
whether that exact image is realized, whether only a different image is
realized, or whether the pair is unrealized altogether. In periodic experiments
a pair can be geometrically realized while the user-supplied shift is still
wrong, and the diagnostic layer should reveal that rather than hide it.

\subsection{Connector-visible support, clearance, and local stability}
\label{subsec:connector_visibility}

Realized-face matching is global, but the specific support notion used later in
Experiment~J4 is narrower and admits an exact local characterization. In the
nonperiodic setting, let
\begin{equation}
    F_{ij}(\weights)=\{x\in\R^d : \pi_i(x)=\pi_j(x)\le \pi_k(x)\ \text{for all } k\neq i,j\}
\end{equation}
denote the unclipped $i$--$j$ power face and let
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{ij}=\{p_i+t(p_j-p_i): t\in\R\}
\end{equation}
denote the connector line and let
\begin{equation}
    H_{ij}(\weights)=\{x\in\R^d : \pi_i(x)=\pi_j(x)\}
\end{equation}
denote the pair bisector. We call $(i,j)$ \emph{connector-visible} if
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{ij}\cap \relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights)\neq\varnothing.
\end{equation}
Here $\relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)}$ means relative interior taken
inside the affine hyperplane $H_{ij}(\weights)$. This is weaker than full
face realization, but it is exactly the support notion
measured by the connector-intersection diagnostics in the molecular section.
The same rowwise formulas hold in the periodic case after fixing the requested
image of $j$; since the molecular benchmark is nonperiodic, we state the result
in that setting. The derivations are short and are deferred to the appendix.

Write
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_{ij}=p_j-p_i,\qquad
    D_{ij}=\lVert \Delta_{ij}\rVert^2,\qquad
    x_{ij}(t)=p_i+t\Delta_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The connector line meets the pair bisector at the unique point
\begin{equation}
    x_{ij}(\weights)=x_{ij}(t_{ij}(\weights)),\qquad
    t_{ij}(\weights)=\frac12+\frac{w_i-w_j}{2D_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
For each competitor $k\neq i,j$, decompose
\begin{equation}
    p_k-p_i=u_{ijk}\Delta_{ij}+e_{ijk},\qquad e_{ijk}\perp \Delta_{ij},
\end{equation}
and define the competitor slack at the connector-bisector point by
\begin{equation}
    g_{ijk}(\weights)=\pi_k(x_{ij}(\weights))-\pi_i(x_{ij}(\weights)).
\end{equation}
A direct calculation gives the affine law
\begin{equation}
    g_{ijk}(\weights)=\lVert e_{ijk}\rVert^2+D_{ij}u_{ijk}(u_{ijk}-1)
    +(1-u_{ijk})w_i+u_{ijk}w_j-w_k.
\end{equation}
Define the row-local visibility score
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{ij}(\weights)=\min_{k\neq i,j} g_{ijk}(\weights),
\end{equation}
with the convention that the minimum over an empty competitor set is $+\infty$.
Thus, in the two-site case, the pair is connector-visible for every finite
weight vector.

\begin{theorem}[Exact connector-visible support criterion]
\label{thm:connector_visibility_main}
Fix a pair $(i,j)$ with $i\neq j$ and assume that no competitor bisector
$H_{ik}(\weights)$ coincides with $H_{ij}(\weights)$ for any $k\neq i,j$.
Then
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{ij}\cap \relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights)\neq \varnothing
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \sigma_{ij}(\weights)>0.
\end{equation}
If $\sigma_{ij}(\weights)<0$, the pair is not connector-visible; if
$\sigma_{ij}(\weights)=0$, the connector-bisector point lies on a higher-order
tie with at least one competitor.
\end{theorem}

The same local geometry yields an explicit margin quantity rather than a purely
visual post hoc score. When $\sigma_{ij}(\weights)>0$, the clearance from the
connector-bisector point to the boundary of the face, measured inside the pair
bisector, is
\begin{equation}
    c_{ij}(\weights)=
    \min_{\substack{k\neq i,j\\ \lVert e_{ijk}\rVert>0}}
    \frac{g_{ijk}(\weights)}{2\lVert e_{ijk}\rVert},
\end{equation}
with the convention that the minimum over an empty index set is $+\infty$.

\begin{corollary}[Clearance formula]
\label{cor:clearance_main}
For a connector-visible pair, $c_{ij}(\weights)$ equals the Euclidean distance
from $x_{ij}(\weights)$ to the nearest blocking competitor hyperplane inside
the pair bisector, with value $+\infty$ if no such hyperplane exists.
\end{corollary}

The molecular computations use a finite padded box rather than the full
unclipped face. Let $B\subset\R^d$ be an axis-aligned box and set
\begin{equation}
    F_{ij}^B(\weights)=F_{ij}(\weights)\cap B.
\end{equation}
Define the signed clipped-box margin by
\begin{equation}
    b_{ij}^B(\weights)=
    \begin{cases}
        \operatorname{dist}_{H_{ij}(\weights)}
        \bigl(x_{ij}(\weights),\partial(H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B)\bigr),
        & x_{ij}(\weights)\in H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B,\\[0.5ex]
        -\operatorname{dist}_{H_{ij}(\weights)}
        \bigl(x_{ij}(\weights),H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B\bigr),
        & x_{ij}(\weights)\notin H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B,
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
with the convention that
$\operatorname{dist}_{H_{ij}(\weights)}(x,\varnothing)=+\infty$. Thus
$b_{ij}^B(\weights)>0$ exactly on
$\operatorname{relint}_{H_{ij}(\weights)}(H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B)$,
$b_{ij}^B(\weights)=0$ on its relative boundary, and
$b_{ij}^B(\weights)<0$ outside the clipped section.

\begin{corollary}[Box-clipped visibility and clearance]
\label{cor:box_clipped_visibility}
The connector-bisector point lies in the relative interior of the clipped face
$F_{ij}^B(\weights)$ inside the pair bisector if and only if
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{ij}(\weights)>0
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    b_{ij}^B(\weights)>0.
\end{equation}
Whenever these inequalities hold, the clipped clearance is
\begin{equation}
    c_{ij}^B(\weights)=\min\{c_{ij}(\weights),\,b_{ij}^B(\weights)\}.
\end{equation}
Thus the padded-box computations used in Experiments~J2--J4 differ from the
unclipped theorem only through the additional box-margin term.
\end{corollary}

When at least one competitor exists, $\sigma_{ij}$ is the minimum of finitely
many affine functions and is therefore globally Lipschitz in the fitted weights.
With
\begin{equation}
    L_{ijk}=|1-u_{ijk}|+|u_{ijk}|+1,
    \qquad
    L_{ij}=\max_{k\neq i,j}L_{ijk},
\end{equation}
we have
\begin{equation}
    |\sigma_{ij}(\weights)-\sigma_{ij}(\widetilde{\weights})|
    \le L_{ij}\lVert \weights-\widetilde{\weights}\rVert_\infty.
\end{equation}
If the competitor set is empty, we set $L_{ij}=0$; the pair is then
connector-visible independently of the weights, and the stability statements
below are trivial for that pair.

\begin{corollary}[Local support stability]
\label{cor:visibility_stability_main}
For a pair with a nonempty competitor set, if
\begin{equation}
    \lVert \weights-\weights^\star\rVert_\infty
    < \frac{|\sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)|}{L_{ij}},
\end{equation}
then the connector-visible status of $(i,j)$ is unchanged between
$\weights^\star$ and $\weights$. If the competitor set is empty, the same
conclusion holds for all finite $\weights$ and $\weights^\star$.
\end{corollary}

For a fixed active row set $A$, the gauge-fixed quadratic fit itself admits an
explicit perturbation bound. Let $\inc_A$, $\mathbf{R}_A$, and $\lap_A$ denote
the row-incidence matrix, diagonal row-weight matrix, and weighted Laplacian
for that active set, and let $Q_A$ have orthonormal columns spanning the chosen
gauge-fixed subspace. The quadratic estimator can then be written as
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\weights}_A = G_A\,\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}_A,
    \qquad
    G_A = Q_A(Q_A^\top \lap_A Q_A)^{-1}Q_A^\top \inc_A\mathbf{R}_A.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Gauge-fixed quadratic perturbation bound]
\label{prop:quadratic_fit_perturbation}
Let $A$ be a fixed informative row set and suppose the observed row targets are
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}_A = \mathbf{z}^{\star}_A + \varepsilon_A,
\end{equation}
with gauge-fixed reference fit $\weights_A^\star = G_A\mathbf{z}^{\star}_A$.
Then
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\weights}_A-\weights_A^\star = G_A\varepsilon_A,
    \qquad
    \lVert \widehat{\weights}_A-\weights_A^\star\rVert_\infty
    \le C_A\lVert \varepsilon_A\rVert_\infty,
\end{equation}
where $C_A=\lVert G_A\rVert_{\infty\to\infty}$.
\end{proposition}

This turns the local support criterion into a genuine estimator-to-support
bridge for the quadratic inner solve. The same identity also yields an
energy-norm and graph-spectral interpretation of fit stability.

\begin{corollary}[Graph-spectral perturbation and support stability]
\label{cor:quadratic_fit_spectral}
Let $e_A=\widehat{\weights}_A-\weights_A^\star$ and define the Laplacian
energy norm
\begin{equation}
    |e_A|_{\lap_A}:=(e_A^\top \lap_A e_A)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
    |e_A|_{\lap_A}
    \le \lVert \mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\varepsilon_A\rVert_2,
    \qquad
    \lVert e_A\rVert_2
    \le \lambda_{\min}^+(\lap_A)^{-1/2}\,\lVert \mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\varepsilon_A\rVert_2,
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_{\min}^+(\lap_A)$ denotes the smallest positive eigenvalue of
$\lap_A$ on the chosen gauge-fixed subspace. Consequently, for a pair with a
nonempty competitor set, if
\begin{equation}
    L_{ij}\,\lambda_{\min}^+(\lap_A)^{-1/2}\,\lVert \mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\varepsilon_A\rVert_2
    < |\sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)|,
\end{equation}
then the connector-visible status of $(i,j)$ is unchanged between
$\weights_A^\star$ and $\widehat{\weights}_A$. Pairs with empty competitor sets
are again weight-independent and hence stable trivially.
\end{corollary}

A larger gauge-fixed spectral gap therefore improves the stability radius of
both the fitted weights and the local support diagnostic.

\begin{corollary}[One-step identification from the full candidate fit]
\label{cor:one_step_visibility}
Let $A=\candpairs$ be the initial full candidate row set and let
\begin{equation}
    \realpairs^\star_{\mathrm{vis}}
    =\{(i,j)\in\candpairs : \sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)>0\}.
\end{equation}
Assume that every candidate pair under discussion has a nonempty competitor set
(the empty-competitor two-site case is already weight-independent), and assume
\begin{equation}
    \gamma=\min_{(i,j)\in\candpairs}|\sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)|>0,
    \qquad
    L=\max_{(i,j)\in\candpairs}L_{ij},
\end{equation}
and let $C_{\candpairs}$ be the perturbation constant from
Proposition~\ref{prop:quadratic_fit_perturbation} for the candidate-set
quadratic fit. If
\begin{equation}
    C_{\candpairs}\,\lVert \varepsilon_{\candpairs}\rVert_\infty < \frac{\gamma}{L},
\end{equation}
then the local prune map computed from the full candidate fit,
\begin{equation}
    T(\candpairs)=\{(i,j)\in\candpairs : \sigma_{ij}(\widehat{\weights}_{\candpairs})>0\},
\end{equation}
recovers the connector-visible support exactly:
\begin{equation}
    T(\candpairs)=\realpairs^\star_{\mathrm{vis}}.
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

These statements upgrade the molecular visibility/clearance layer from an
empirical mask to a theorem-backed local support diagnostic. What they do
\emph{not} do is solve the full outer algorithm. Full face realization,
periodic-image bookkeeping, and hysteretic active-set behavior remain global
computations, and the molecular benchmark still uses clipped faces inside a
finite padded box rather than the idealized full-space face. Experiment~J4
therefore includes a dedicated box-margin sensitivity check so that the
additional clipping term can be tested directly on the reported molecular
subset.

\subsection{Self-consistent active-set refinement}
The outer algorithm refines a Boolean active mask over the full candidate row
set. Starting from an initial active mask (all rows active by default), each
iteration fits only the active rows, evaluates which candidate rows are
geometrically supported in the resulting tessellation, records connector-based
visibility diagnostics when requested, and then updates the active mask by
hysteretic add/drop rules. The algorithm never invents new candidate pairs and
never silently changes a user-supplied periodic image.

The add/drop thresholds provide hysteresis against one-iteration flips, while
optional under-relaxation reduces oscillations in nearly marginal cases. The
explicit max-iteration cap, cycle-window test, and weight-step tolerance are
implementation safeguards rather than theorem-backed stopping rules. The
returned diagnostics record, for every candidate row, whether it ended active or
inactive, whether it was ever realized, how often its active/realized status
toggled, and whether it belongs to a detected cycle.

\paragraph{What is theorem-backed and what remains empirical}
The fixed-row inverse problem is analyzed mathematically in
Sections~\ref{sec:consistency} and \ref{sec:inversefit}. The connector-visible
score $\sigma_{ij}$, its clearance $c_{ij}$, the spectral perturbation
corollary, and the one-step margin corollary above are also theorem-backed.
What remains empirical is the global wrapper that combines these local
quantities with full tessellation matching, periodic image logic, and
under-relaxed hysteretic updates. We therefore do not claim universal
convergence of the outer loop. The point of Algorithm~\ref{alg:selfconsistent}
is narrower: it organizes support checking around quantities whose local
geometric meaning is explicit and whose failure modes can be diagnosed. In
addition, the Boolean hysteresis itself has a finite local stabilization
property: once the evaluated diagnostic mask remains fixed for
$h=\max(A_{\mathrm{add}},A_{\mathrm{drop}})$ consecutive iterations, the active
mask equals that stable mask after at most $h$ updates, and a subsequent small
aligned step triggers the existing self-consistency test
(see \ref{app:hysteresis_stabilization}).

Algorithm~\ref{alg:selfconsistent} summarizes the practical refinement loop.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\small
\caption{Self-consistent inverse fitting on candidate rows}
\label{alg:selfconsistent}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require Sites, resolved candidate rows, domain, model, initial active mask $a^{(0)}$, options $(A_{\mathrm{add}},A_{\mathrm{drop}},\omega_{\max},K_{\mathrm{cyc}},\tau_w)$
\Statep{Initialize add/drop streak counters, a table of recently seen active masks, and previous evaluation weights as empty}
\For{$k=1,\dots,\omega_{\max}$}
    \Statep{Fit weights on the currently active rows using the low-level solver}
    \If{the inner fit is infeasible or terminates numerically}
        \Statep{terminate with the corresponding diagnostic status}
    \EndIf
    \If{previous evaluation weights exist}
        \Statep{align the new weights componentwise to the previous gauge and, if enabled, under-relax before realization matching}
    \EndIf
    \Statep{Build the induced power tessellation and match realized boundaries for all candidate rows}
    \Statep{Record requested-shift realization, other-shift realization, and connector-based visibility/clearance diagnostics as needed}
    \Statep{Update per-row add/drop streak counters and propose a new active mask}
    \If{the proposed mask equals the current mask, the realized-same-shift mask equals the current mask, and the weight-step norm is at most $\tau_w$}
        \Statep{terminate as self-consistent}
    \ElsIf{the proposed mask reappears within the last $K_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ iterations}
        \Statep{terminate with a cycle diagnostic and report marginal rows}
    \EndIf
    \Statep{Accept the proposed mask and continue}
\EndFor
\Statep{terminate with max-iteration status}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Numerical experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Benchmark blocks, common protocol, and metrics}
The numerical study has two layers. The first is a compact synthetic suite:
Experiments~A--D test the fixed-row theory under exact supports, dense noise,
random row loss, and hard equality or interval restrictions, while
Experiments~E--H test the realization-aware workflow under strict supergraphs,
periodic image ambiguity, and controlled wrong-model stress. The second layer
is the molecular QTAIM benchmark (Experiment~J), which uses QTAIM molecular
graph support extracted from 100 optimized molecules to test true-support
separator quality, recovery from a generic excessive graph, and
representability-aware diagnostics. Detailed A--H tables remain in the
appendix and reproducibility archive. A smaller surrogate benchmark based on
separators extracted from non-power partitions is retained in the archive as
additional wrong-model stress; the molecular branch provides the external
validation reported in the main text.

Unless otherwise stated, synthetic instances are generated in axis-aligned box
domains $D=[0,1]^d$ for $d\in\{2,3\}$, with periodic counterparts obtained by
identifying opposite faces. For Experiments~A--H, exact separator fractions are
first extracted from realized interfaces of a ground-truth power diagram and
are then perturbed according to the benchmark family. The molecular benchmark
instead starts from fixed molecular geometries, extracts QTAIM bond critical
points through Multiwfn \cite{LuChen2012,Lu2024Multiwfn}, projects those points
to atom--atom connector lines to obtain separator proxies, and records the BCP
off-axis distance as a quality flag. Separator RMSE/MAE are reported in the
native synthetic fraction units for Experiments~A--H and in \AA{} for the
molecular benchmark. Additional graph-aware summaries report support
precision/recall/F1, connectivity or shift diagnostics, and bonded/nonbonded
splits when they are materially informative.

\subsection{Compact synthetic validation}
The synthetic section provides controlled validation: it checks that the
proposed inverse and realization-aware machinery behaves as expected under
idealized stress cases, while the molecular branch supplies the main external
validation. Table~\ref{tab:synthetic_summary} gives the main quantitative
anchors. The fixed-row controls (Experiments~A--D) follow the theory:
compatible data are recovered to machine precision, dense noise degrades
smoothly, graph disconnection removes only the global anchoring, and short
infeasible cycles are detected by the difference-constraint reduction. The
missing-data transition in Figure~\ref{fig:missing_data_transition} is the
most compact visual check of that identifiability story: within-component
recovery stays exact, while global alignment returns only after the observed
graph reconnects.

The realization-aware controls (Experiments~E--H) identify both the value and
the limits of the outer diagnostic layer. Under strict supergraphs, repeated
fit--check--prune cycles improve true-row separator fit much more reliably than
they improve support F1. Under periodic wrong-image corruption, shift-aware
repair removes a distinct realized-other-shift signature. Under structured
wrong-model stress, the workflow remains useful as diagnostic cleanup, but not
as a universal support-recovery upgrade: true-edge separator RMSE usually
improves, whereas support F1 is mixed and projection residual often rises
because refinement trades algebraic fit against realized consistency. The
archived surrogate benchmark based on separators extracted from non-power
partitions points in the same direction, so those results remain in the
archive rather than in the main numerical narrative.

\subsection{Molecular QTAIM benchmark (Experiment~J)}
The molecular branch is the main empirical section of the paper. It
benchmarks \emph{pairwise support and separator proxies}, not full QTAIM or
Bader-space reconstruction. The benchmark contains 100 neutral,
closed-shell molecules built under a fixed preprocessing workflow with elements
restricted to H, C, N, O, F, S, and Cl. The acyclic half targets moderate-size
single-component molecules, while the cyclic half deliberately introduces more
topologically complex systems. The resulting QTAIM graphs are therefore neither
trivial bond networks nor hand-picked chemistry showcases.

\subsubsection{J1 dataset audit}
Table~\ref{tab:j1_dataset_audit} shows why this dataset is informative.
Higher-order topology is common: 90.0\% of molecules contain at least one ring
critical point and 9.0\% contain at least one cage critical point. Nonbonded
BCP-supported edges appear in 84.0\% of molecules, so the benchmark is not
just recovering obvious covalent graphs. The key split for later
interpretation, however, is bonded versus nonbonded proxy quality. Bonded BCPs
are highly connector-aligned: their median off-axis distance is 0.0045~\AA{},
and 98.2\% project within 0.02~\AA{} of the connector. Nonbonded BCPs define a
much harsher regime: their median off-axis distance is 0.151~\AA{}, and only
1.1\% fall within the same 0.02~\AA{} band (3 of 272 pairs). The pooled
alignment statistics are therefore dominated by the bonded majority rather than
by uniformly clean supervision. Projected BCPs remain useful pairwise support
proxies, while the nonbonded subset functions as a support-diagnostic stress
test rather than clean separator supervision. Detailed graph-distance
stratification is provided in the archived exports; here we use the
bonded/nonbonded split because it is the dominant proxy-quality distinction.

\subsubsection{J2 true-support separator ladder}
The true-support ladder asks a narrower question than graph recovery: on
QTAIM-supported pairs, how good are the separator targets themselves? Ordinary
Voronoi is the zero-signal baseline of the same inverse-fitting framework: if
every candidate row is assigned the midpoint target $t=\frac12$, then all
implied weight differences vanish and the fitted diagram is ordinary Voronoi up
to gauge. The J2 panel of Table~\ref{tab:j23_reference_summary} therefore
reports successive layers of the same fitting ladder rather than unrelated
geometries. Because the nonbonded proxy is usually far off-axis before fitting
begins, J2 is a clean connector-supervised separator benchmark on bonded
support and a mixed proxy-/representability stress test on nonbonded support.
To keep the subset logic explicit, we distinguish the broad heteronuclear
subset, the low-offset subset defined by projected BCP offset at most
0.02~\AA{}, and their intersection, the low-offset heteronuclear subset.

On all QTAIM-supported edges, the frozen proatomic rule reduces RMSE from
0.1332 to 0.0452~\AA{} and closes 75.7\% of the midpoint-to-ceiling gap.
One-shot powerfit reconciliation moves those targets only slightly
(proatomic-to-powerfit distortion RMSE 0.0114~\AA{}) and does not improve
true-support RMSE. The quantitative split in
Table~\ref{tab:j23_reference_summary} shows where the remaining headroom lies:
on bonded edges the support-conditioned ceiling is 0.0062~\AA{} versus
0.0457~\AA{} for the proatomic rule, whereas on nonbonded edges the ceiling
itself is 0.0605~\AA{} and is already worse than the proatomic rule
(0.0372~\AA{}). The nonbonded separator ladder is therefore informative about
difficulty, but it is not a clean connector-supervised reconstruction test:
proxy mismatch and fixed-site representability limits are already entangled
before the one-shot fit is applied. The full heteronuclear subset remains
harder (Table~\ref{tab:j23_reference_summary}), but the model class is not
intrinsically too crude when supervision is clean: on the low-offset
heteronuclear subset, the support-conditioned ceiling is 0.0060~\AA{} and every
edge is realized. The main role of powerfit is therefore not to refine already
strong local targets on the true support; it is to reconcile them into one
globally consistent weighted tessellation and to expose which parts of the
recovered support are geometrically credible.


\subsubsection{J3 recovery from a generic excessive graph}
J3 is the main support-recovery experiment. Because all three tested distance
cutoffs (4.0, 4.5, and 5.0~\AA{}) already cover 100\% of QTAIM-supported
edges, the experiment measures supergraph compression rather than recall
rescue. The candidate graph itself is therefore the right first baseline. At
the reference 5.0~\AA{} cutoff,
candidate precision is only 0.102 and candidate F1 is only 0.185 overall. Raw
recovered support lifts F1 to 0.339, and visibility-masked recovered support
lifts it further to 0.821. On bonded edges the visible recovered graph is
essentially exact (precision 0.9995, recall 1.0000, F1 0.9997); on nonbonded
edges it is still much weaker (precision 0.1351, recall 0.9926, F1 0.2379).
Because 93.2\% of true supported edges are bonded, the overall micro-F1 is
dominated by broad bonded support. The hard regime is the nonbonded tail:
true-edge coverage is 1.000 at every cutoff, but the deliberately excessive
supergraph leaves many surplus nonbonded candidates after visibility masking.
Thus the nonbonded columns measure support selection under off-axis proxy
supervision and fixed-site representability limits, not precise nonbonded
separator reconstruction.

\subsubsection{J4 local diagnostics, representative geometry, and mask transfer}
J4 addresses a different question from J3. Experiment~J3 evaluates support
recovery against the fixed QTAIM benchmark graph of 3975 supported edges
(3703 bonded and 272 nonbonded). Experiment~J4 instead studies the local
geometry of faces that a given model actually realizes: conditional on a
realized face, do the visibility and clearance diagnostics separate
QTAIM-supported faces from surplus realized faces? The J4 scores are therefore
computed within each model's realized-face archive rather than against a
single global denominator. Within that realized-face setting, the visibility
mask has a precise geometric meaning: by
\cref{thm:connector_visibility_main}, a pair is connector-visible exactly when
its connector line intersects the relative interior of the fitted face inside
the pair bisector, and by
\cref{cor:clearance_main} the clearance is the distance from that
connector-bisector point to the nearest blocking competitor within the pair
bisector. The actual molecular computations use clipped faces in a padded
5.0~\AA{} box, and \cref{cor:box_clipped_visibility} shows that clipping enters
only through the additional box-margin term $b_{ij}^B$. In the released
archive, recovered powerfit realizes all 3975 QTAIM-supported edges, whereas
Voronoi realizes 3973, including 270 of the 272 nonbonded edges; the
difference is confined to two hard cyclic nonbonded cases and does not affect
the qualitative conclusions below.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three conclusions stand out. First, on a fixed row set the inverse problem is
a graph-structured difference problem: compatibility, gauge, identifiability,
and hard restrictions reduce to incidence, Laplacian, and Bellman--Ford
structure on the row-indexed multigraph, and Experiments~A--D confirm the
numerical regimes predicted by that theory.

Second, the gap between algebraic fit and support recovery is no longer a
featureless heuristic layer. For the connector-line notion used in the
molecular study, visibility and clearance admit explicit row-local formulas,
perturbation bounds, a clipped-box extension, and a one-step
fit--check--prune corollary. On the released J4 archive, the clipped analytic
diagnostics coincide with the tessellation-based outputs up to machine
precision, so the local theorem is computationally validated rather than merely
interpretive. What remains empirical is the global wrapper that combines those
diagnostics with full tessellation matching, periodic-image bookkeeping, and
hysteretic updates. Experiments~E--H therefore justify it as practical cleanup,
not as a proved global algorithm.

Third, the molecular benchmark becomes much easier to interpret once those
layers are separated. The frozen proatomic law already provides strong local
targets on the true support, ordinary Voronoi is the zero-signal baseline on
the same inverse-fitting ladder, and powerfit's main job is to reconcile those
targets into a consistent weighted geometry. On the reference 5.0~\AA{}
candidate graph, visibility-masked recovered support reaches precision 0.697
and F1 0.821 overall and is essentially exact on bonded edges. The remaining
difficulty lies in nonbonded pairs, where proxy mismatch and model-class limits
are already entangled. There the relevant claim is conditional rather than
global: within each model's realized-face archive, clearance is best used as a
ranking diagnostic, and on visible nonbonded faces it materially outperforms
face area.

The limitations are specific. The molecular study does not attempt full QTAIM
or Bader reconstruction, and projected BCPs are only pairwise separator proxies
with an explicit off-axis quality flag. That flag is highly heterogeneous:
bonded support is strongly connector-aligned, whereas connector-aligned
nonbonded supervision is almost absent in the present dataset. The chemistry
scope is restricted to neutral closed-shell molecules containing H, C, N, O,
F, S, and Cl under one fixed workflow. The local theorem addresses nonperiodic
connector visibility rather than full face realization. Periodic image
handling, domain clipping, and full hysteretic outer-loop behavior therefore
remain algorithmic layers rather than solved theory.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We formulated fixed-site inverse fitting of power-diagram weights from sparse
pairwise separator data and showed that, on a fixed row set, the problem
reduces to graph-structured compatibility, gauge, identifiability, and hard-
restriction feasibility on a row-indexed constraint multigraph. In the
quadratic case, the estimator is a gauge-fixed Laplacian solve.

For the support layer, the connector-line diagnostic admits explicit local
geometry. Competitor slacks at the connector-bisector point are affine in the
fitted weights; under the noncoincidence assumption of
\cref{thm:connector_visibility_main}, connector visibility is equivalent to
positivity of the row-local slack score, the clearance of a connector-visible
pair is an explicit distance-to-blocker formula, and clipping in the padded-box
setting enters only through an additional box-margin term. On
the released J4 archive, these clipped analytic quantities reproduce the
implementation's visibility and clearance diagnostics up to machine precision.
The quadratic fit also admits a graph-spectral perturbation corollary, and
under a margin condition the local support layer yields a one-step
fit--check--prune result; full outer-loop convergence remains outside the
paper's claims.

The numerical evidence is equally focused. Synthetic controls confirm the
fixed-row theory and the practical role of realization-aware cleanup under
strict supergraphs, periodic ambiguity, and controlled wrong-model stress. On
the molecular QTAIM benchmark, the frozen proatomic law closes 75.7\% of the
midpoint-to-ceiling separator gap on true support, recovery from a generic
5.0~\AA{} candidate graph reaches visible-support F1 $=0.821$ overall and
$0.9997$ on bonded edges, and the remaining mismatch is concentrated in a
proxy- and representability-limited visible nonbonded regime where, within
each model's realized-face archive, clearance is informative as a ranking
diagnostic.

Taken together, these results identify a tractable fixed-site inverse-fitting
regime: sparse pairwise separator data can be reconciled into a globally
consistent weighted geometry, while explicit local diagnostics separate
algebraic fit from support credibility on a nontrivial molecular benchmark.

\section{Connector-visible support: formulas and proofs}
\label{app:connector_visibility}

This appendix proves the local support statements from
Section~\ref{subsec:connector_visibility}. We work in the nonperiodic setting
and use the same notation as in that section. The formulas describe the
unclipped pair face in full space; they are therefore local geometric
statements rather than claims about domain clipping.

\begin{proposition}[Unique connector--bisector point]
\label{prop:connector_line_point}
For every pair $(i,j)$ with $i\neq j$, the connector line $\ell_{ij}$ meets the
pair bisector $\{x : \pi_i(x)=\pi_j(x)\}$ at the unique point
\begin{equation}
    x_{ij}(\weights)=x_{ij}(t_{ij}(\weights)),
    \qquad
    t_{ij}(\weights)=\frac12+\frac{w_i-w_j}{2D_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substituting $x_{ij}(t)=p_i+t(p_j-p_i)$ into $\pi_i(x)=\pi_j(x)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \|t(p_j-p_i)\|^2-w_i=\|(1-t)(p_j-p_i)\|^2-w_j.
\end{equation}
Since $D_{ij}=\|p_j-p_i\|^2>0$, this reduces to
\begin{equation}
    2D_{ij}t=D_{ij}+w_i-w_j,
\end{equation}
which has the unique solution stated above.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Affine competitor slack law]
\label{prop:competitor_slack_affine}
For every competitor $k\neq i,j$,
\begin{equation}
    g_{ijk}(\weights)=\|e_{ijk}\|^2+D_{ij}u_{ijk}(u_{ijk}-1)
    +(1-u_{ijk})w_i+u_{ijk}w_j-w_k.
\end{equation}
In particular, $g_{ijk}$ is affine in the fitted weights.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
At $x=x_{ij}(t)$ we have
\begin{equation}
    x-p_k=(t-u_{ijk})(p_j-p_i)-e_{ijk},
    \qquad
    x-p_i=t(p_j-p_i).
\end{equation}
Because $e_{ijk}\perp (p_j-p_i)$,
\begin{align}
    \pi_k(x)-\pi_i(x)
    &=\|(t-u_{ijk})(p_j-p_i)-e_{ijk}\|^2-w_k
      -\bigl(\|t(p_j-p_i)\|^2-w_i\bigr) \\
    &=\|e_{ijk}\|^2+D_{ij}\bigl((t-u_{ijk})^2-t^2\bigr)+w_i-w_k \\
    &=\|e_{ijk}\|^2+D_{ij}(u_{ijk}^2-2u_{ijk}t)+w_i-w_k.
\end{align}
Substituting the formula from
Proposition~\ref{prop:connector_line_point} gives the claimed affine law.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:connector_visibility_main}]
By Proposition~\ref{prop:connector_line_point}, the connector line meets the
pair bisector $H_{ij}(\weights)$ at exactly one point, namely
$x_{ij}(\weights)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    \ell_{ij}\cap \relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights)\neq\varnothing
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    x_{ij}(\weights)\in\relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights).
\end{equation}
Inside the affine space $H_{ij}(\weights)$, the face is cut out by the
competitor inequalities $\pi_i(x)\le \pi_k(x)$ for all $k\neq i,j$, so
\begin{equation}
    x_{ij}(\weights)\in F_{ij}(\weights)
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    g_{ijk}(\weights)\ge 0\ \text{for all }k\neq i,j.
\end{equation}
If $g_{ijk}(\weights)>0$ for every competitor, continuity gives a relative
neighborhood of $x_{ij}(\weights)$ inside $H_{ij}(\weights)$ on which all of
those inequalities remain strict, so
$x_{ij}(\weights)\in\relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights)$. Conversely,
if $g_{ijk}(\weights)=0$ for some competitor $k$, then the noncoincidence
assumption implies that the restricted affine function
$x\mapsto \pi_k(x)-\pi_i(x)$ is not identically zero on $H_{ij}(\weights)$.
Since it vanishes at $x_{ij}(\weights)$, every relative neighborhood of that
point in $H_{ij}(\weights)$ contains points with $\pi_k(x)-\pi_i(x)<0$, so
$x_{ij}(\weights)\notin\relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
    x_{ij}(\weights)\in\relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights)
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    g_{ijk}(\weights)>0\ \text{for all }k\neq i,j,
\end{equation}
which is exactly the condition $\sigma_{ij}(\weights)>0$. If
$\sigma_{ij}(\weights)<0$, some competitor blocks the connector--bisector point;
if $\sigma_{ij}(\weights)=0$, that point lies on a higher-order tie with at
least one competitor.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:clearance_main}]
Assume $\sigma_{ij}(\weights)>0$. Let $y=x_{ij}(\weights)+h$ with
$h\perp (p_j-p_i)$, so that $y$ remains in the pair bisector. For the affine
function $\delta_{ijk}(x)=\pi_k(x)-\pi_i(x)$,
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \delta_{ijk}(x)=2(p_i-p_k)=-2\bigl(u_{ijk}(p_j-p_i)+e_{ijk}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{ijk}(y)=\delta_{ijk}(x_{ij}(\weights))+
    \langle \nabla\delta_{ijk},h\rangle
    =g_{ijk}(\weights)-2\langle e_{ijk},h\rangle.
\end{equation}
If $\|e_{ijk}\|>0$, the blocker $\delta_{ijk}(y)=0$ is therefore a hyperplane in
that bisector with normal $e_{ijk}$, and its distance from $x_{ij}(\weights)$
is $g_{ijk}(\weights)/(2\|e_{ijk}\|)$. If there is at least one competitor with
$\|e_{ijk}\|>0$, the boundary of the face inside the pair bisector is the union
of these blocking hyperplanes, so the clearance is their minimum distance. If
there is no such competitor, then every competitor inequality is constant on
$H_{ij}(\weights)$; since $\sigma_{ij}(\weights)>0$, they are all strictly
satisfied everywhere on that bisector, so the face has no boundary inside
$H_{ij}(\weights)$ and the clearance is $+\infty$. This matches the empty-index
convention in the stated formula for $c_{ij}(\weights)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:box_clipped_visibility}]
Inside the affine space $H_{ij}(\weights)$, both the unclipped face
$F_{ij}(\weights)$ and the clipped section $H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B$ are convex.
Their intersection is exactly the clipped face $F_{ij}^B(\weights)$. The
connector-bisector point therefore lies in the relative interior of the clipped
face if and only if it lies in the relative interior of both factors:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    x_{ij}(\weights)\in \operatorname{relint}_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}^B(\weights)
    &\Longleftrightarrow
    x_{ij}(\weights)\in\relint_{H_{ij}(\weights)} F_{ij}(\weights) \\
    &\phantom{\Longleftrightarrow}\qquad \text{and} \\
    &\phantom{\Longleftrightarrow}
    x_{ij}(\weights)\in\operatorname{relint}_{H_{ij}(\weights)}(H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The first condition is equivalent to $\sigma_{ij}(\weights)>0$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:connector_visibility_main}. For the clipped section
$S=H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B$, the signed definition of $b_{ij}^B(\weights)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    b_{ij}^B(\weights)>0
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    x_{ij}(\weights)\in\operatorname{relint}_{H_{ij}(\weights)} S.
\end{equation}
Thus the second condition is equivalent to $b_{ij}^B(\weights)>0$, which proves
the visibility statement.

For the clearance identity, let $r_F=c_{ij}(\weights)$ and
$r_B=b_{ij}^B(\weights)$. By Corollary~\ref{cor:clearance_main}, the closed
ball in the pair bisector centered at $x_{ij}(\weights)$ with radius $r_F$ is
the largest such ball contained in the unclipped face. When
$b_{ij}^B(\weights)>0$, the same signed-distance definition shows that the
analogous largest ball contained in $H_{ij}(\weights)\cap B$ has radius $r_B$.
A centered in-plane ball is contained in the clipped face iff it is contained
in both sets, so the largest admissible radius for the intersection is
$\min\{r_F,r_B\}$. This is exactly $c_{ij}^B(\weights)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:visibility_stability_main}]
If the competitor set is empty, connector visibility is independent of the
weights by the convention above. Otherwise, by
Proposition~\ref{prop:competitor_slack_affine}, the coefficient vector of
$g_{ijk}(\weights)$ has entries $(1-u_{ijk})$ on $i$, $u_{ijk}$ on $j$, $-1$ on
$k$, and zero elsewhere. Its $\ell_1$ norm is therefore
$L_{ijk}=|1-u_{ijk}|+|u_{ijk}|+1$, so for every competitor,
\begin{equation}
    |g_{ijk}(\weights)-g_{ijk}(\weights^\star)|
    \le L_{ijk}\,\|\weights-\weights^\star\|_\infty
    \le L_{ij}\,\|\weights-\weights^\star\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
Taking minima over $k$ preserves the same Lipschitz bound:
\begin{equation}
    |\sigma_{ij}(\weights)-\sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)|
    \le L_{ij}\,\|\weights-\weights^\star\|_\infty.
\end{equation}
If the right-hand side is strictly smaller than
$|\sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)|$, then $\sigma_{ij}$ cannot cross zero and its
sign is preserved. By Theorem~\ref{thm:connector_visibility_main}, the
connector-visible status is unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:quadratic_fit_perturbation}]
Write the gauge-fixed weights as $\weights=Q_A\theta$. On that subspace the
quadratic objective becomes
\begin{equation}
    \frac12\bigl\|\mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}(\inc_A^\top Q_A\theta - \mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}_A)\bigr\|_2^2.
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to $\theta$ gives the reduced normal equations
\begin{equation}
    (Q_A^\top \lap_A Q_A)\widehat{\theta}_A
    =Q_A^\top \inc_A\mathbf{R}_A\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}_A.
\end{equation}
By construction the gauge is fixed on the informative graph, so
$Q_A^\top \lap_A Q_A$ is invertible. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\theta}_A
    =(Q_A^\top \lap_A Q_A)^{-1}Q_A^\top \inc_A\mathbf{R}_A\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}_A,
\end{equation}
and multiplying by $Q_A$ yields
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\weights}_A
    =Q_A(Q_A^\top \lap_A Q_A)^{-1}Q_A^\top \inc_A\mathbf{R}_A\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}_A
    =G_A\mathbf{z}^{\mathrm{obs}}_A.
\end{equation}
Applying the same formula to the reference targets $\mathbf{z}^{\star}_A$ gives
$\weights_A^\star=G_A\mathbf{z}^{\star}_A$. Subtracting the two identities shows
that
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{\weights}_A-\weights_A^\star = G_A\varepsilon_A.
\end{equation}
Taking $\ell_\infty$ norms and using the induced matrix norm proves the stated
bound with $C_A=\|G_A\|_{\infty\to\infty}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:quadratic_fit_spectral}]
Let $e_A=\widehat{\weights}_A-\weights_A^\star$ and write
$e_A=Q_A(\widehat{\theta}_A-\theta_A^\star)$. Subtracting the reduced normal
equations for $\widehat{\theta}_A$ and $\theta_A^\star$ gives
\begin{equation}
    Q_A^\top \inc_A\mathbf{R}_A(\inc_A^\top e_A-\varepsilon_A)=0.
\end{equation}
Left-multiplying by $(\widehat{\theta}_A-\theta_A^\star)^\top$ and using
$e_A=Q_A(\widehat{\theta}_A-\theta_A^\star)$ yields
\begin{equation}
    e_A^\top \lap_A e_A
    = e_A^\top \inc_A\mathbf{R}_A\varepsilon_A
    = (\mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\inc_A^\top e_A)^\top(\mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\varepsilon_A).
\end{equation}
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
\begin{equation}
    |e_A|_{\lap_A}^2
    \le |e_A|_{\lap_A}\,\lVert \mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\varepsilon_A\rVert_2,
\end{equation}
so either $e_A=0$ or division by $|e_A|_{\lap_A}$ gives
\begin{equation}
    |e_A|_{\lap_A}
    \le \lVert \mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\varepsilon_A\rVert_2.
\end{equation}
Because $e_A$ lies in the chosen gauge-fixed subspace, the Rayleigh quotient on
that subspace implies
\begin{equation}
    e_A^\top \lap_A e_A
    \ge \lambda_{\min}^+(\lap_A)\,\lVert e_A\rVert_2^2,
\end{equation}
whence
\begin{equation}
    \lVert e_A\rVert_2
    \le \lambda_{\min}^+(\lap_A)^{-1/2}|e_A|_{\lap_A}
    \le \lambda_{\min}^+(\lap_A)^{-1/2}\,\lVert \mathbf{R}_A^{1/2}\varepsilon_A\rVert_2.
\end{equation}
Finally, $\lVert e_A\rVert_\infty\le \lVert e_A\rVert_2$, so the visibility
implication follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:visibility_stability_main}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:one_step_visibility}]
For the full candidate fit, Proposition~\ref{prop:quadratic_fit_perturbation}
gives
\begin{equation}
    \|\widehat{\weights}_{\candpairs}-\weights^\star\|_\infty
    \le C_{\candpairs}\,\|\varepsilon_{\candpairs}\|_\infty
    < \frac{\gamma}{L}
    \le \frac{|\sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)|}{L_{ij}}.
\end{equation}
Corollary~\ref{cor:visibility_stability_main} therefore preserves the sign of
$\sigma_{ij}$ for every candidate pair. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{ij}(\widehat{\weights}_{\candpairs})>0
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \sigma_{ij}(\weights^\star)>0
\end{equation}
for every $(i,j)\in\candpairs$, which is exactly the statement
$T(\candpairs)=\realpairs^\star_{\mathrm{vis}}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Local stabilization of the hysteretic mask}
\label{app:hysteresis_stabilization}
The implementation uses a rowwise hysteretic Boolean update: each row is added
once it has been proposed for activation during $A_{\mathrm{add}}$
consecutive evaluations, dropped once it has been proposed for removal during
$A_{\mathrm{drop}}$ consecutive evaluations, and otherwise retains its current
state. The following proposition isolates the finite-memory part of that rule.

\begin{proposition}[Finite stabilization of the rowwise hysteresis]
\label{prop:hysteresis_stabilization}
Let $v^{(k)}\in\{0,1\}^m$ be the Boolean diagnostic mask evaluated at
iteration $k$, let $a^{(k)}\in\{0,1\}^m$ be the active mask after the $k$th
hysteretic update, and set
\begin{equation}
    h=\max(A_{\mathrm{add}},A_{\mathrm{drop}}).
\end{equation}
If there exist $k_0\ge 0$ and $v^\star\in\{0,1\}^m$ such that
\begin{equation}
    v^{(k)}=v^\star,
    \qquad k=k_0+1,\dots,k_0+h,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
    a^{(k_0+h)}=v^\star,
\end{equation}
regardless of the initial active mask and the current add/drop streak counters.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Local mask stabilization and termination]
\label{cor:hysteresis_stabilization}
Suppose there exist a reference weight vector $\weights^\star$, a radius
$\delta>0$, and a mask $v^\star\in\{0,1\}^m$ such that the evaluated Boolean
diagnostic satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \lVert \weights-\weights^\star\rVert_\infty<\delta
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    v(\weights)=v^\star.
\end{equation}
If the evaluated weights produced by Algorithm~\ref{alg:selfconsistent} remain
inside that ball for $h$ consecutive iterations, then after at most $h$ updates
its active mask equals $v^\star$. If, on the next iteration, the realized-
same-shift mask also equals $v^\star$ and the aligned weight-step norm is at
most $\tau_w$, then Algorithm~\ref{alg:selfconsistent} satisfies its
self-consistency termination test at that iteration.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:hysteresis_stabilization}]
The update is rowwise, so it suffices to consider one row $r$. If
$v_r^\star=1$, then during the $h$ stated iterations the add streak for row $r$
increments at every step and the drop streak resets to zero. After at most
$A_{\mathrm{add}}\le h$ consecutive ones, row $r$ is activated and remains
active because the evaluated diagnostic never returns to zero on that window.
If $v_r^\star=0$, the same argument with the roles of add/drop exchanged shows
that after at most $A_{\mathrm{drop}}\le h$ consecutive zeros, row $r$ is
dropped and remains inactive. Thus every row matches $v_r^\star$ by iteration
$k_0+h$, so the whole active mask equals $v^\star$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:hysteresis_stabilization}]
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:hysteresis_stabilization} to the diagnostic sequence
$v^{(k)}=v(\weights^{(k)})$. The neighborhood assumption makes that sequence
constant and equal to $v^\star$ throughout the stated $h$-step window, so the
active mask equals $v^\star$ after at most $h$ updates. If the next iteration
also has realized-same-shift mask $v^\star$ and aligned weight-step norm at
most $\tau_w$, then all three Boolean conditions in the self-consistency test
of Algorithm~\ref{alg:selfconsistent} are satisfied and the algorithm
terminates with its self-consistent status.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Open-segment and periodic variants]
The implemented molecular diagnostic is connector-line visibility, not the
stronger requirement that the connector--bisector point lie between the two
sites. If the open-segment version is desired, one adds
$0<t_{ij}(\weights)<1$ to the criterion from
Theorem~\ref{thm:connector_visibility_main}. For a resolved periodic row, the
same derivation applies after replacing $p_j$ by the requested image of site
$j$; what changes is the global bookkeeping of admissible images rather than the
row-local algebra.
\end{remark}
